Abstract

Training loss tells us whether a model improves, but rarely explains how its internal computation changes. The nanoGPT Observable Library connects recorded weights, gradients, activations, and attention statistics to the modules that produced them.

Researchers can move through the architecture, compare the same signal across layers or setups, zoom into a transition, and preserve the result as a public discovery. The goal is simple: gain more research intuition from observables.

Why more observables?

The phenomena we know are bounded by what we routinely measure.

Grokking and scaling laws have already shown that training contains structure far richer than a smooth fall in loss. Yet most training dashboards still return to a small set of familiar signals: loss, accuracy, learning rate, and parameter norms.

Our premise is that more phenomena are waiting in observables we inspect less often. Attention entropy is one example. In this run it exposes a three-regime sequence in Block 1 that is not recoverable from a standard weight norm alone.

A hidden phase structure

Block 1 undergoes a sequence of phase transitions.

Mean attention entropy measures how diffuse attention is across key positions. In the baseline run, Block 1 first pauses near 4, drops to a second plateau near 2, then rises toward 2.5. These plateau-to-plateau phase transitions are unique to this block in the baseline run.

Block 1 is zero-indexed: it is the second transformer block.

Compare depth

The other blocks do not take the same path.

Overlaying all 12 blocks on shared axes turns the unusual shape into a depth-wise comparison. Block 1 is highlighted; the remaining blocks are drawn in neutral tones.

Compare setups

Warmup changes the order of the dynamics.

Hold Block 1 and the observable fixed, then change the training setup. The comparison isolates two controlled changes: model depth and learning-rate warmup.

Close the feedback loop

See something unexpected? Put the evidence where others can inspect it.

You do not need to leave the observation in a private screenshot or guess where to send it. Choose the path that matches how developed the finding is.

The library will keep growing.

Next we plan to add more training setups and record runs for more steps at finer intervals, making transitions easier to resolve and compare.

Over time, the library can also grow into a lightweight experiment platform: inspect a signal, form a hypothesis, and test a small intervention without leaving the same interface.
