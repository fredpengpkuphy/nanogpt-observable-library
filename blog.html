Abstract

Training loss tells us whether a model improves, but rarely explains how its internal computation changes. The nanoGPT Observable Library connects recorded weights, gradients, activations, and attention statistics to the modules that produced them.

Researchers can move through the architecture, compare the same signal across layers or setups, zoom into a transition, and preserve the result as a public discovery. The goal is simple: make unusual internal dynamics easy to see and easy to turn into research questions.

Why observables

A final metric cannot show the path learning took.

Two models can reach a similar loss while their layers organize in very different ways. One layer may change smoothly; another may pause, collapse, or reorganize long after the headline metric stops looking interesting.

Those dynamics are usually scattered across logs and notebooks. The Observable Library keeps them attached to the architecture and to training time, so a strange curve is not an isolated image—it is evidence that can be compared with the rest of the model.

A small observation

Block 1 moves through three entropy regimes.

Mean attention entropy measures how diffuse attention is across key positions. In the baseline run, Block 1 first pauses near 4, drops to a second plateau near 2, then rises toward 2.5. The curve in Figure 01 makes the stages visible; comparison tells us whether they are special.

Block 1 is zero-indexed: it is the second transformer block.

Compare depth

The other blocks do not take the same path.

Overlaying all 12 blocks on shared axes turns the unusual shape into a depth-wise comparison. Block 1 is highlighted; the remaining blocks are drawn in neutral tones.

Compare setups

Warmup changes the order of the dynamics.

Hold Block 1 and the observable fixed, then change the training setup. The three trajectories are qualitatively different, not small variations of one shared curve.

Questions to test

The repeated levels near 2 and 4 may be attractor-like regimes, and warmup may help entropy decrease more stably. Block 0 also loses entropy much faster without warmup, which may act like an early reduction in effective context length. These are prompts for experiments, not conclusions.

Looking forward

Make the next observation easier to find.

The curves do not yet explain the mechanism behind these transitions. They do something important first: localize the event, show that it is layer-specific, and reveal which setup changes its character.

That is what the Observable Library is for—turning the path of learning into inspectable evidence, then turning evidence into a better question.
