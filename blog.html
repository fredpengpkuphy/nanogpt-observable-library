01 / Motivation

A loss curve is a summary, not an explanation

Two models can reach similar loss while their layers organize in very different ways. Those differences are easy to miss when weights, gradients, activations, and attention are buried in separate logs.

We built this site as a microscope for training: connect internal measurements to the architecture, keep training time visible, and make comparisons cheap enough that an odd curve can become a research question.

03 / A small demo

One layer moves through three regimes

Consider attention_entropy_mean, a measure of how diffuse attention is across key positions. In the baseline run, Block 1 pauses near 4, drops to a second plateau near 2, then rises toward 2.5.

Block 1 is zero-indexed: it is the second transformer block.

Reading the metric. Higher values mean more diffuse attention; lower values mean more concentrated attention.

04 / Compare in place

The pattern becomes clear only in context

A strange curve is more useful when we can ask whether it is specific to one layer or one setup. These are the comparisons the Explorer is designed to make directly.

Two guesses to test

The repeated levels near 2 and 4 may be attractor-like regimes, and warmup may help attention entropy descend more stably instead of first collapsing to 2 and rebounding to 4. Block 0 also loses entropy much faster without warmup, which may behave like an early reduction in effective context length. These are prompts for experiments, not conclusions.
